01

Awakening Billions of Sensors

The vision and mission of FIA Operating Systems
Jump to topic

I am Neksha DeSilva, a researcher at FIA Operating Systems. Today, I am not just presenting a new piece of hardware; I am introducing a fundamental paradigm shift in how digital systems interpret the physical world.

We were founded to address a universal, silent crisis: the collapse of sensor value. Every sensor currently in existence, regardless of whether it is a sophisticated biomedical instrument, a heavy engineering gauge, a precision meteorological device, a vibration sensor, or a simple ultraviolet photodiode, is on an unpredictable trajectory toward obsolescence. They all depreciate. The defining mission of FIA Operating Systems is to fundamentally reduce the slope of this depreciation for absolutely all sensor types.

Our technology is a hardware-agnostic, cross-platform software, powered by pure mathematical logic rather than reliance on specific silicon chips. We have proven that this same intelligent logic can run on a powerful 64-bit Intel i9 laptop, it can be integrated into custom Linux firmware, and yet it is lean enough to operate effectively on an 8-bit microcontroller like an Arduino Uno.

This extreme agnosticism means our framework is universal. It means we could partner with automotive giants like Mercedes Benz, Volkswagen, or Porsche to integrate this intelligent firmware directly into their Android-based multimedia players. We are democratizing access to this data, moving away from fragile, dust-prone, $2000 industry-standard pyranometers toward a future where anyone can build the same device for $2 at home.

Silicon Labs Si7021 Deprecation

Silicon Labs Si70xx series end-of-life in January 2025
Jump to topic

The consequence of this stagnation is best illustrated by the Silicon Labs Si7021. For years, this was a dominant sensor in the mid-tier consumer market. However, in January 2025, Silicon Labs officially deprecated the entire Si70xx series.

While official channels often cite European Union regulatory restrictions as the driver for this decision, our research suggests a more fundamental market shift.

If you look at the longitudinal analysis tracking the maker ecosystem from 2009 to 2015, we see a distinct trend: massive initial adoption followed by a hard plateau. The market reached a 'value limit.' A sensor capable of measuring only static humidity and temperature simply could not evolve to meet the complexity of modern applications.

The Si7021 didn't fail because it broke; it was deprecated because the hardware could no longer scale in value.

Physical Implementation and Reference Architecture

Reference Node, Flux Node, and Main System
Jump to topic

To validate a software-defined physics engine, you need a physical reference. This is the FiaPhy Reference Architecture.

This setup allows us to execute the Method of Differential Sensing. It consists of three critical components working in unison:

The Reference Node: This is our 'Ground Truth.' It is an exposed, accumulative temperature stress catcher. It interacts directly with the ambient air to determine the baseline environmental state.

The Flux Node: This is the counter-point. It is shielded and isolated, designed to react differently to energy inputs than the reference.

The Main System: This box is the brain. It houses the Physics Engine, the networking stack, and the feedback loops that process the raw data from the two nodes into usable energy metrics.

Multi-Sensor Array Topology

Using sensor arrays for improved accuracy
Jump to topic

To ensure meteorological-grade precision using commodity hardware, we do not rely on a single data point. Instead, we implement a multi-sensor array topology.

The system incorporates multiple reference and flux sensors running in parallel. By maintaining an equal number of nodes on each side, we can perform statistical averaging across the array. This approach mitigates individual sensor noise and hardware variances, resulting in a data stream that is significantly more stable and accurate than any single component could achieve on its own.

12

RP2040 Dual Core Architecture

$0.80 processor with bifurcated processing model
Jump to topic

The computational core of the system is the RP2040. We selected this processor for its specific architecture and cost-efficiency.

At approximately $0.80 per unit, it provides a Dual Core ARM architecture running at 133 MHz, which allows us to implement a bifurcated processing model.

Core 1 is dedicated to the Physics Engine. Its priority is running the thermodynamic calculations and Inertial Noise Reduction without interruption.

Core 2 handles the FiaPhy API, Wi-Fi connectivity, and storage management.

This hardware separation ensures that network activity or peripheral management never introduces latency into the real-time physics computations.

Sustainable Energy Architecture

Self-sustaining power compartment design
Jump to topic

A sensor that dies is a sensor that fails its purpose. To achieve our vision of awakening billions of sensors, we cannot rely on grid power or frequent battery replacements.

Therefore, the power architecture of the FiaPhy system is designed for indefinite, self-sustaining operation. We are not just plugging in a battery; we are engineering an energy harvesting ecosystem.

The Power Compartment is designed to act as a thermodynamic engine. The lid is entirely black, an intentional physical constraint. Black surfaces maximize radiation absorption. We want the lid to get hot. This creates a deliberate thermal contrast with the body of the enclosure, which is designed to remain cooler through ventilation.

15

Dual-Architecture Hybrid Power System

Solar panel + Thermoelectric Generator (TEG)
Jump to topic

We utilize a Dual-Architecture Hybrid Power System.

The primary source is a standard 60x100mm Photovoltaic Solar Panel. But solar panels have a known inefficiency: they generate significant 'waste heat' during operation, reaching upwards of 65°C to 72°C.

We turn that waste into an asset. We have bonded a Thermoelectric Generator (TEG) directly underneath the solar panel using metal-to-metal conduction. As the solar panel heats up, that thermal energy conducts directly into the TEG before dissipating into the cooler interior heat sink.

Using the Seebeck effect, the TEG converts that thermal gradient back into electricity. We are essentially recycling the solar panel's own heat loss to extend the battery life of the device.

16

Offline Storage and Data Recovery

EEPROM backup and automatic recovery logic
Jump to topic

Power means nothing without data integrity. In real-world deployments, connectivity is never guaranteed.

The system monitors connectivity in real-time. If the Wi-Fi link fails, the logic immediately diverts the data stream to the local EEPROM. It creates a packet containing the timestamp, the calculated flux, and the raw variables.

The system continues to log locally until the connection is re-established. Once online, a recovery routine triggers, automatically uploading the 'gap' in the data. This ensures that even if the network goes dark, the scientific record remains unbroken.

The EEPROM can store up to 24 hours of backup data, more than 8000 data points, ensuring continuous operation even during extended connectivity issues.

17

Embedded Web Server Architecture

Asynchronous API endpoints and self-hosting
Jump to topic

Having covered the physical sensing and power systems, we now turn to how the system actually communicates.

This device is engineered to function not just as a data logger, but as an asynchronous web host. It is designed to manage multiple concurrent API endpoints.

The /api/data endpoint serves real-time thermodynamic packets to any connected client in JSON format.

The /api/recover endpoint handles data continuity; it automatically streams the buffered offline data from the EEPROM the moment a connection is re-established.

The /api/wifi endpoint allows the device to accept new credentials dynamically, meaning it can be reconfigured for different networks without reflashing the code.

This architecture ensures the device is self-sufficient, managing its own user interface, error handling, and data recovery processes locally.

Hardware Agnosticism Verification

32-bit RP2040 vs 8-bit Arduino Uno ATmega328P
Jump to topic

I previously stated that FiaPhy is a hardware-independent framework. This means that whether you run it on a high-performance 64-bit system or a simple 8-bit microcontroller, the physics engine maintains the exact same accuracy.

To prove this, we took the FiaPhy firmware to the IntelliHacks machine learning competition at the University of Colombo School of Computing. Thank you to Dr. Jehan for giving us the platform to demonstrate this technology in front of the faculty and undergraduate students.

For this demonstration, we stripped away the advanced hardware. We used a standard Arduino Uno connected to two basic DHT11 sensors and a 16x2 LCD display.

This board uses the ATmega328P, which is a significantly lower-tier 8-bit processor compared to the 32-bit ARM architecture in our main device. Because of this constraint, we cannot process data at the same 300-millisecond speed as the FiaPhy ARM architecture. We have slightly increased the sampling interval to cope with the 8-bit limitations, but the mathematical accuracy remains identical.

As the code executes, you can see the outputs in real-time on the LCD. Notice that the Heat Flux remains at absolute zero. This is scientifically correct, as we are inside a conference room with no solar load. However, you can see the Solar Radiation metric reacting. It is detecting the energy from a red light we focused on the sensors to simulate a radiative event.

This verifies that the software-defined physics engine is running successfully on standard, entry-level hardware.

Modern environmental monitoring systems, from automotive platforms to industrial installations, often rely on sensing architectures decades old. While sensor hardware has plateaued, algorithmic processing potential has grown exponentially. This creates an engineering challenge as billions of functional sensors are discarded annually, perceived as incapable of advanced measurements like solar radiation or heat flux.

This technology mitigates hardware obsolescence by transferring computation from physical components to intelligent software. By upcycling commodity sensors into high-precision radiometric instruments without firmware changes, it preserves global sensor infrastructure utility in modern autonomous frameworks.

The core is the Differential Temporal Derivative Soft-Sensing (DTDSS) framework, replacing expensive and fragile specialized equipment like pyranometers which are prohibitive in autonomous systems. It uses a differential thermodynamic architecture with paired sensors: a Reference Node alongside a Flux Node to isolate active energy exchanges from ambient conditions. An Inertial Noise Reduction (INR) filter compensates for thermal inertia, predicting equilibrium temperatures in real time.

Unlike machine learning approaches, this system uses physics-based derivations. Air density and enthalpy are calculated via first-principles thermodynamics, ensuring accuracy across altitudes and climates. The state machine is optimized for extreme efficiency, requiring under 60 bytes of RAM, compatible with 8-bit microcontrollers.

FIA Operating Systems is a network of scientists, developers, and industry professionals advancing hardware-agnostic solutions for global sustainability. This ensures established innovations continue contributing to a data-rich, sustainable future.

Billions of sensors exist measuring simple parameters only because that is how they were labeled. The true value of mathematical approximation is often overlooked. A combination of such sensors can yield derived parameters, values that cannot be obtained directly with perfect precision. However, perfect precision is not always required.

Restricting sensor potential limits future generations from building upon current work. Sensors, old or new, built for various purposes, when combined together, can derive far more advanced real-world parameters in applications that do not demand absolute precision. FiaPhy is a research initiative focused on maximizing the potential of sensors, the near-future carriers of full-human automation, to help sustain existing and upcoming innovations while enabling them to work with these frameworks for generations to come.

Temporal Derivative Soft-Sensing and Reconstructing Solar Radiation and Heat Flux from Common Environmental Sensors and FiaOS Engineering

Neksha V. DeSilva
Independent researcher, FiaOS.org | Dated: November 25, 2025
Subjects: Environmental Engineering; Soft-Sensing; Solar Radiation (physics.ao-ph); IoT Systems
Summarized Abstract

Current environmental monitoring is fundamentally limited by a reliance on static state variables, temperature, pressure, and humidity, while remaining blind to the dynamic energy exchanges that drive them. This paper introduces Differential Temporal Derivative Soft-Sensing (DTDSS), a novel physics-based framework that transforms standard, low-cost environmental sensors into capability-dense radiometers. By employing a differential topology with Inertial Noise Reduction (INR), we can mathematically reconstruct Global Horizontal Irradiance (GHI) and convective heat flux without the cost or fragility of thermopile pyranometers. While validated using the FiaOS reference architecture, this methodology is hardware-agnostic. The ultimate vision of this work is the deployment of a high-performance, open-source computational library designed for professional embedded development environments. By distributing this algorithm via global repositories, we aim to upgrade the capabilities of millions of existing and future IoT devices. This library allows standard electronics to move beyond simple linear measurements and unlock higher-order environmental physics. By deriving complex energy flux parameters from common sensors, we open a new frontier of derived equations and applications, from precision agriculture to autonomous energy management, essentially democratizing advanced meteorological physics for the global engineering community.
